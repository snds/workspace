# Research: Industry, Markets and Policy

Most industry evidence is published by someone who wants something. Read for the interest first.

## Field profile

- **Grey literature:** reports outside peer review, of every quality. Appraise with AACODS
  ([[research-source-appraisal]]).
- **Marketing as evidence:** case studies, "trusted by" logos and customer quotes are claims by the
  seller.
- **Paywalled data:** analyst numbers get quoted without methods. Find the method or mark it Tier 4.
- **Fast drift:** prices, features, market share and regulations change; every fact needs an *as
  of* ([[dated-claims]]).

## Where the better evidence is

| Need | Prefer | Over |
|---|---|---|
| What a product does | Its docs, changelog, a trial | Its marketing page |
| Company facts | Filings (annual reports, regulatory filings) | Press coverage |
| Rules | The regulator or standards body's text | Summaries of it |
| Market size | Methodology-disclosed reports, several | One headline number |
| What customers think | Many independent reviews over time | Selected testimonials |

## Landscape scans

Scan **by vertical**, not as one market (#04: what fashion PLM tools do matters for fashion work).
For each player: what it does (from primary material), for whom, since when, and the date checked.
Cross-vertical patterns are Tier 2 evidence for a specific vertical, not Tier 1.

## Mistakes to avoid

- A vendor's case study cited as independent evidence.
- A market-size number with no method.
- A feature comparison with no dates.
- One vertical's pattern applied to another as if proved.
