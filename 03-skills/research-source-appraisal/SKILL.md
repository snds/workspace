# Research Source Appraisal

Stage 6 of [[19-research-operating-model]]. A source earns weight; it doesn't arrive with it.

## The five questions, for every source

1. **Kind:** primary, secondary or tertiary ([[research-foundations]] §2)? Peer-reviewed, preprint,
   official, grey, personal?
2. **Origin:** where did *this* source get the claim? Trace it one link back, further if it matters.
3. **Maker:** who, with what expertise, funded by whom, wanting what?
4. **Status:** current, corrected, retracted, superseded? Check the publisher's page and Retraction
   Watch for papers; changelogs for docs; later editions for books.
5. **Date and independence:** when, and is it echoing a source you already have?

## Open web: read laterally

Fact-checkers judge a page by leaving it: open new tabs and see what others say about the source
before reading it closely (Wineburg and McGrew's lateral reading). Caulfield's **SIFT** makes that a
habit: **Stop**; **Investigate** the source; **Find** better coverage; **Trace** claims, quotes and
media to their original context.

## Studies: know the level

Rank by design before reading results: systematic reviews of good trials > single trials > cohort
studies > case reports > expert opinion (Oxford CEBM levels). A higher level badly done can lose to a
lower level done well; appraise the method, not the label. Statistical validity defers to #15.

## Grey literature: AACODS

For reports, white papers, theses, policy documents (Tyndall 2010): **Authority** (who wrote it),
**Accuracy** (methods, references), **Coverage** (limits stated), **Objectivity** (interests),
**Date**, **Significance** (does it add anything).

## Red flags

- A claim no one else reports, from a source with something to sell.
- A paper in a journal that charges to publish and reviews nothing (check DOAJ membership and the
  publisher's record).
- A statistic with no traceable origin, repeated widely.
- A quote with no date, place or original.
- A screenshot instead of a link.

## Record the appraisal

One line per kept source in the finding's method section: kind, maker, date, status, why kept. A
dropped source gets a reason too, if it was prominent.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Judging a page by its design, its "About" page or its domain ending.
- Treating "peer-reviewed" as "true".
- Missing that a famous paper was retracted or failed to replicate.
- Counting a source and its press coverage as two.
