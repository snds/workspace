# Quotes and copyright in research

**The rule:** quote rarely and briefly: **no quote over 15 words**, always cited, always in
quotation marks. Everything else in the workspace's own words, without copying a source's structure
or sequence. This is the house standard behind #19 §4 gate 5 and the `research.finding` harness;
`09-tools/research-check.py` measures it.

## Why a hard number

Fair use and fair dealing are judged case by case, weighing purpose, the nature of the work, how
much is taken and the effect on its market (the four factors in US law, 17 U.S.C. §107; the US
Copyright Office's Fair Use Index collects the cases). A short, cited quote that supports analysis
sits at the safe end of every factor. A fixed limit removes the judgment call from routine work.

## In practice

- **Quote** when the exact words matter: a definition, a claim you'll dispute, a line whose
  wording is the evidence.
- **Paraphrase** everything else, and still cite it. Close paraphrase (the source's sentences with
  words swapped) is copying.
- **One quote per source** in a finding, as a rule of thumb.
- **Song lyrics, poems, code and data:** quote less, link more. Code snippets follow the project's
  licence.
- **Images and figures:** link or describe; reproduce only with a licence that allows it.

## Not legal advice

This is a working standard for research notes and deliverables, not a legal opinion. Publishing
anything commercially, or quoting at length for a reason, needs the publisher's or a lawyer's view.
