    What this means

    What it is. Import's careful read of your workspace before it brings it in. Claude Code reads your workspace's files in 9 batches, grouped by area, and lists the problems the free rule checks can't see: rules that contradict each other, skills that overlap or never load, out-of-date facts, unclear instructions. The rule checks already ran.

    What it touches. A read-only copy of your workspace, taken at commit 2ff55d4 and kept on your Mac. Each batch can only read files. It can't change anything, run commands, or reach the internet apart from the model.

    What you get. On the Import screen, each finding with its evidence and a proposed fix, to accept, edit or reject. Then a new copy of your workspace with those fixes, scored on the exit tests. Your real workspace doesn't change unless you later choose to merge.

    Why it waits for you. It runs on your Claude Code sign-in, so it counts against your Claude usage: about 5.8 million tokens in all. Each batch stops at $10 of usage or 30 minutes, and you set a cap for the whole run.

    The deny list comes first. A plain text file of words that must never leave your Mac: your employer's name, client and product names, private host names. The scan's summary is checked against it before it's written, and held back if a word appears. One word or phrase per line, in ~/Library/Application Support/design.snds.sy/deny-terms.txt.

    Then the other runs. A second scan of the same copy, to see whether the two agree (C8). Scans of the ten test workspaces Create made (C9). An agent doing real tasks in your workspace and in the imported copy, compared (C10).

    What to say. Once the list exists: "Go on the deep scan, with a total cap of $N."

    What this means

    What it is. A new background program that is the only part of the app ever holding a password or token. git, gh, SSH and agents get the right account for the repo or project they're in, decided by rules you write once. Today that's held together by hand: separate gh folders, git rules per remote, and switching back after every login.

    What you get, in order. First Watch: a warning before a token expires or a commit goes out under the wrong name, holding no secrets. Then Broker: the right identity for git, gh and SSH, personal accounts first. Then the gateway: agents use both Linear workspaces, Jira and GitHub through the right account without ever seeing a token. Beside them: two installs of one app tied to two personas, sealed facts for sensitive specifics, and the Integrations screen with externals.

    Size. About 19 focused weeks in 19 items. The roadmap said 10–15 because it counted the access phases alone.

    What it asks of you. An Apple team (it unlocks the signature check that employer accounts need, D110); a private GitHub App under your account for sign-in (D112); one sign-in per account as each part lands; Windows PC time; two weeks of daily use on all three devices.
